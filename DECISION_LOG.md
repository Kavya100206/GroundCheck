1. **SpotifyCares chosen over AmazonHelp and AppleSupport** — Real computed numbers confirmed the working hypothesis: AmazonHelp fails intent-diversity criterion (too broad for 4–6 intent cap, disqualifying regardless of resolution rate); AppleSupport fails visible-resolution criterion (30.8% DM-redirect + link-dump replies).

2. **English-only filter applied** — Non-English traffic present in SpotifyCares inbound data (~4% by langdetect; likely overstated, as "Love u", "Just sent you a DM", "Android 7.0" were misclassified as non-English by langdetect on short texts). Filter is justified by existing project scope decision. Logged here so the denominator discrepancy is not discovered later.

3. **31,213 raw thread count is not the usable pool** — 22,148 threads (71%) have no captured inbound tweet via the original `in_response_to` filter, because root @mention customer messages are not linked via that chain. An alternate join (brand outbound `in_response_to_tweet_id` → customer root tweet in full dataset) recovered **28,576 additional English threads**. See Decision log entry 6.

4. **Taxonomy capped at 5 intents** — Within the 4–6 target; a 6th intent ("Connect/Disconnect") was considered while reading the data and rejected because it collapses cleanly into `device_platform` or `playback_issue` depending on whether the stated complaint is audio quality or app function. Expanding would add labelling burden without improving coverage.

5. **Visible-resolution heuristic limitation acknowledged** — The heuristic (reply length ≥ 80 chars AND no DM-redirect phrase) does not catch phone/email redirects (relevant for AmazonHelp, less so for SpotifyCares). For SpotifyCares, the sampled non-DM replies confirm genuine troubleshooting content, so the 72.0% estimate is treated as reliable for this brand.

6. **Alternate join check: 28,576 additional English threads folded into usable pool** — Investigated whether brand outbound `in_response_to_tweet_id` could recover root customer tweets missing from the original filter. Result: 30,084 net new recoverable inbound tweets, 28,576 English-only. Join quality verified on 10-pair spot-check — all pairs coherent (customer complaint clearly matches brand reply). Gain was substantial (~4× pool expansion), well above the "marginal" threshold; folded in. Effective English usable pool: **~37,267 threads** (8,691 original + 28,576 recovered). Note: some recovered tweets are thin ("Hi please see my DM") — a minimum text-length filter at sampling time screens these out.

7. **Playback bucket keyword heuristic recalibrated due to 50% early contamination** — During Phase 1b golden set labelling, the first 10 rows of the `playback_issue` bucket yielded 5 `other` labels (50% contamination), triggering the agreed >10% contamination threshold. Root cause: the original keyword heuristic matched broad music nouns (`song`, `track`, `album`, `podcast`, `artist`) without requiring a malfunction indicator, pulling in feature requests ("add a random song button", "translating songs"), content availability inquiries ("how do you choose a podcast"), and metadata questions ("exact release date"). The heuristic was rebuilt with two gates: (a) explicit exclusion of feature-request patterns (`add a`, `feature`, `could you`, `request`, `suggestion`, `why don't you have`, etc.), and (b) inclusion restricted to explicit playback failure terms (`skip`, `stutter`, `buffer`, `freeze`, `crash`, `won't play`, `stuck`, `lag`, `stops playing`, `cuts out`, `no sound`, `distorted`). The candidate pool tightened from 10,471 to 979 high-precision failure candidates (466 easy, 513 hard). Surviving rows: 4 genuine `playback_issue` rows from the first 10 (all easy). Replacement plan: 41 fresh candidates sampled (27 easy, 14 hard) to restore the 45-row stratum at the committed 31 easy / 14 hard allocation.

8. **Golden set target revised from 200 to 150 examples** — Trimmed proportionally across all five intents (`playback_issue`: 34, `app_technical`: 30, `account_login`: 30, `device_platform`: 30, `premium_billing`: 26) to manage human labelling fatigue risk while preserving the exact stratification shape and the ~70% easy / ~30% hard split (105 easy, 45 hard). 150 remains fully within the assignment's explicit 150–250 range. The 23 genuine `playback_issue` rows already hand-labelled were preserved intact; only unlabelled rows from the tail of each bucket were trimmed (50 unlabelled rows archived to `trimmed_unlabelled_rows_pool.csv`). Zero labelled rows were dropped.

9. **Overall `other` rate shifted from legacy artifact to active cumulative signal at row 94** — Cumulative `other` rate across all labelled data reached 15.0% (15/100, including 5 archived rows) and 10.6% in active set alone (10/94). Critically, 10 of the 15 are live `other` labels surfaced during real labelling (8 in `app_technical`, 2 in `account_login`), rather than legacy noise alone. Agreed reconciliation policy: do not re-sample mid-stream to force exact 34/30/30/30/26 strata matching; complete Batch 2 (56 rows), evaluate final emergent distribution across all 150 rows, and only trigger targeted top-up if any core intent ends up critically thin (<15–18 examples). Monitor `other` rate through Batch 2, stopping to investigate only if it climbs meaningfully past 15% with fresh out-of-taxonomy rows from `device_platform` or `premium_billing`.

10. **`app_technical` heuristic bleed: distinct failure mode from `playback_issue` and standby top-up filter prepared** — Analysis of the `app_technical` stratum (rows 35–64) identified a distinct heuristic failure mechanism from Entry 7. While `playback_issue` suffered from *false-positive-into-other* (feature requests with no failure language), `app_technical` suffered from *false-positive-into-adjacent-real-intents*: broad terms like `"download"`, `"update"`, `"offline mode"`, `"freeze"`, and `"glitch"` bled heavily into `playback_issue` (10 rows), `device_platform` (3 rows), and `account_login` (2 rows), while non-technical download queries (e.g. publisher track stats) became `other` (8 rows), yielding only 7 genuine `app_technical` rows from its 30-row stratum (11 cumulative). Rather than disrupting Batch 2, a tightened standby filter was constructed and verified: targeting explicit application-layer failures (`app won't open/launch/load`, `black/blank screen`, `clean reinstall`, `clear cache`, `error code [N]`) while strictly excluding audio nouns, login terms, and billing phrases. The standby pool yields 152 fresh high-precision candidates, primed for instant top-up during post-Batch 2 reconciliation if `app_technical` ends up below the 15–18 floor.

11. **Batch 2 contamination (third instance): taxonomy-wide heuristic permeability confirmed** — Final labelling of Batch 2 (56 rows across `device_platform` and `premium_billing`) revealed 7 additional feature-request/wishlist rows matching keyword filters without describing malfunctions (gs_0094, gs_0097, gs_0105, gs_0113, gs_0117, gs_0124, gs_0140). Unlike Entry 7 (`playback_issue`) and Entry 10 (`app_technical`), this contamination was distributed across multiple buckets rather than isolated to one, confirming that keyword heuristic permeability is a systemic, taxonomy-wide characteristic of the twcs dataset rather than an intent-specific flaw. Active set `other` rate closed at 12.7% (19/150), within the 15% threshold; cumulative rate (including 5 archived rows) closed at 15.4% (24/156), validating Entry 9's prediction that the `other` signal is a genuine property of real user support traffic.

12. **Difficulty-split deviation: planned ~70/30 easy/hard, emergent 67.3% hard / 32.7% easy (nearly inverted)** — The sampling plan originally targeted ~70% easy / ~30% hard cases based on pre-sampling heuristics (text length, multi-keyword matches, escalation keywords). However, rigorous hand-labelling across the 150-row golden set revealed an actual distribution of **67.3% hard (101/150) and 32.7% easy (49/150)** — nearly an inversion of the target. Root cause: real inbound customer tweets directed to support are intrinsically elliptical, fragmented, emotionally charged, multi-faceted, or lacking essential context (e.g. missing device/OS, vague error descriptions, concurrent complaints), requiring escalation or non-trivial diagnostic routing. Surface keyword heuristics severely underestimated real-world ambiguity and customer complexity. Critically, this deviation is an honest emergent finding that serves as **good news for Phase 5's LLM-as-judge validation**: having 101 hard cases (versus the originally planned 45) substantially strengthens the judge validation by rigorously stressing human-judge agreement on ambiguous edge cases where eval trust actually matters, rather than artificially inflating agreement on trivial, obvious examples. It is a genuine empirical finding that strengthens evaluation rigour, not merely a deviation to apologize for.

13. **Architectural policy for `other` rows: Option A locked (classification on 132 in-taxonomy, escalation on full 151)** — The classifier output space is strictly capped at 5 intents per Phase 1 commitments (`playback_issue`, `app_technical`, `account_login`, `device_platform`, `premium_billing`). Hand-labelling produced 19 rows gold-labelled `other` (12.6% of the 151-row golden set), reflecting real out-of-taxonomy customer traffic (feature requests, wishlist demands, general feedback). Option A was formally selected as the locked architectural choice:
    - **Intent Classification Metric:** Evaluated strictly on the **132 in-taxonomy rows** (`playback_issue`: 52, `account_login`: 27, `premium_billing`: 23, `app_technical`: 15, `device_platform`: 15). The 5-intent classifier cannot predict `other` by definition; penalizing it for out-of-taxonomy inputs would distort per-intent calibration without testing intent disambiguation.
    - **Escalation Decision Metric:** Evaluated on the **full 151 rows** (including all 19 `other` cases). Real deployments inevitably receive noise and out-of-scope requests; what matters for system safety and trust is that the agent recognizes its own boundary and does not auto-handle unclassifiable requests with irrelevant canned technical troubleshooting. Safely escalating an out-of-taxonomy message is a true system success, while auto-handling it is a safety failure.

14. **Phase 2 baselines established and locked in prior to agent construction** — Three distinct baselines were implemented in `src/baselines.py` and evaluated against the finalized 151-row golden set (strictly excluding all golden IDs from training and retrieval pools):
    - **Baseline 1 (Trivial Floor):** Majority-class intent (`playback_issue`) achieved **39.39%** accuracy (52/132 in-tax). Always-escalate policy achieved **45.03%** accuracy (68/151 full set), with **0.0% False Auto-Handles (FAH = 0/68)** and **100.0% False Escalations (FE = 83/83)** — setting the absolute safety floor (zero missed escalations, zero automation value).
    - **Baseline 2 (Classification-Only):** Word + bigram TF-IDF with Logistic Regression trained on 1,250 balanced silver-labelled tweets from the unsampled pool achieved **66.67%** in-taxonomy accuracy (88/132), with macro F1 = 0.630 and weighted F1 = 0.686. Per-intent F1s: `account_login` = 0.786, `premium_billing` = 0.762, `playback_issue` = 0.750, `device_platform` = 0.478, `app_technical` = 0.375. Confirms that simple n-gram features handle domain-distinct complaints well, but suffer from significant vocabulary overlap between application crashes, platform compatibility, and audio playback.
    - **Baseline 3 (Simple Competitive Retrieval + Rule Gate):** Evaluated top-1 nearest neighbor TF-IDF retrieval over 8,310 historical visible-resolution threads, achieving a **Top-1 Retrieval Intent Match Rate of 57.58%** (distinguished from direct classification accuracy, as it measures whether retrieved evidence happens to share the same intent). Paired with deterministic hard gates (billing, security, legal), the escalation cutoff was swept across similarity thresholds $\tau \in [0.20, 0.50]$. At balanced $\tau = 0.35$, overall escalation accuracy was **53.0%** (Easy: 56.0%, Hard: 51.5%), with FAH = 27/68 (39.7%) and FE = 44/83 (53.0%). At safety-biased $\tau = 0.50$, FAH fell to 6/68 (8.8%), but FE climbed to 76/83 (91.6%).
    - **Heuristic noise caveat for Baselines 2 & 3:** Both baselines rely on training/retrieval corpora indexed using the same keyword heuristics demonstrated in Phase 1b (Entries 7, 10, 11) to suffer from permeability and label noise. Consequently, these baseline figures are conservative estimates of what "simple, done carefully" could achieve with human-curated silver data, not an absolute upper ceiling.
    - **Informative hurdle established:** Beating Baseline 2 requires an LLM classifier that can genuinely disambiguate nuanced failure contexts beyond surface keyword presence (especially `app_technical` and `device_platform`). Beating Baseline 3 on escalation requires a calibrated confidence boundary that reduces False Auto-Handles without collapsing into trivial over-escalation.

15. **Baseline 3 threshold locked at $\tau = 0.35$ as the sole official evaluation hurdle** — To prevent post-hoc baseline selection (i.e. retrospectively picking whichever $\tau$ threshold flatters the final agent's results in Phase 4 or Phase 6), **$\tau = 0.35$ is formally locked as the single official Baseline 3 operational point**. Rationale: $\tau = 0.35$ represents the genuinely competitive, balanced baseline (overall escalation accuracy 53.0%, Easy accuracy 56.0%, Hard accuracy 51.5%, FAH 39.7%, FE 53.0%). The conservative $\tau = 0.50$ configuration (45.7% accuracy, 8.8% FAH, 91.6% FE) is nearly indistinguishable from Baseline 1's trivial always-escalate policy, making it an uninformative hurdle. Locking $\tau = 0.35$ before any agent code is written ensures the evaluation benchmark is honest, fixed, and unmanipulable. $\tau = 0.50$ is preserved solely as supplementary context on the threshold calibration tradeoff.

16. **LLM classifier pinned model selection (`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`), single-model evaluation, and strict retrieval index alignment** — Resolved before Phase 4 to maintain strict reproducibility, auditability, and groundedness alignment:
    - **Single Pinned Model Resolution:** The planned primary model (`llama-3.3-70b-versatile`) was unavailable on this Groq API key tier (HTTP 404). While an exploratory run briefly tested `groq/compound-mini`, that system was formally rejected: it is an agentic tool-calling "compound" system rather than a standard deterministic chat-completion model, and it hit a 250 RPD daily quota limit. To ensure complete scientific reproducibility, **`qwen/qwen3.8-27b` is permanently pinned as the sole model** for classification and reply generation. A clean, single-model evaluation was executed across all 132 in-taxonomy golden set rows using pure chat completions (temperature 0.0, zero tool-calling wrappers) with zero errors.
    - **Single-Model Classification Performance vs Baseline 2:** Under pure `qwen/qwen3.8-27b`, the classifier achieved **82.58% overall accuracy** (109/132) on the in-taxonomy golden set, decisively beating Baseline 2's hurdle of **66.67%** (+15.91 percentage points). Macro F1 reached **0.819** (vs. Baseline 2's 0.630, +0.189). Weighted F1 reached **0.830** (vs. Baseline 2's 0.686, +0.144). Per-intent breakdown: `account_login` = 0.929 F1 (26/27 recall), `premium_billing` = 0.913 F1 (21/23 recall), `playback_issue` = 0.805 F1 (35/52 recall), `device_platform` = 0.765 F1 (13/15 recall), and `app_technical` = 0.683 F1 (14/15 recall). It maintains high robustness on ambiguous cases with **83.84% accuracy on the hard tier** (83/99).
    - **Retrieval Index Rebuilt on Strict Baseline 3 Criteria:** The initial 26,218-document index in `src/retrieval.py` had omitted the silver-intent requirement, retaining unassigned chatter and conversational replies (66.8% of pool). The index was completely rebuilt to enforce the exact Baseline 3 strict visible-resolution criteria: brand reply length $\ge 80$ characters, absence of naive DM-redirect phrases, and an unambiguous operational intent (`silver_intent.notna()`). This yields **8,310 documents** (~21.2% of the unsampled training pool), perfectly matching Baseline 3's 8,310 corpus and the golden set's audited 22.0% visible-resolution rate, ensuring that the 22%/78% groundedness split is preserved intact for Phase 4 and Phase 6. *(Boundary note: The initial 3-document discrepancy of 8,313 vs 8,310 was an artifact of unescaped internal newlines inside two multi-line customer tweets [IDs 289165 and 2203694] parsed as phantom rows upon CSV reload; sanitizing embedded newlines aligns the stored corpus to exactly 8,310 documents).*

17. **Retrieval spot-check metric shift: 90% 'relevant' to 30% 'concrete actionable resolution' as an internal audit precedent** — When the 20-query manual retrieval spot-check was first run, it was categorized as '18/20 (90%) relevant'. However, closer inspection revealed that this 'relevance' was driven by topical proximity (e.g. matching a Windows DLL complaint to another Windows DLL complaint) where the historical brand reply was actually a subtle DM-redirect ('sent a DM your way', 4 rows), a diagnostic question ('What device and OS are you using?', 8 rows), or a generic status update ('we're looking into this', 2 rows). Only 6 of the 20 queries (30%) retrieved a **concrete actionable resolution** (e.g. clean reinstall steps, student discount link, restart sequence):
    - **Internal Audit / Misleading Number Precedent:** This metric inflation is a prime, real-world example of the exact 'misleading number' mechanism that Phase 6's report section is intended to expose: conflating *topical semantic relevance* with *operational grounding resolution*. Catching and correcting this internally rather than presenting an inflated 90% grounding claim ensures the system remains trustworthy.
    - **Permanent Policy:** Going forward, the retrieval audit strictly uses a four-way mutually exclusive categorization scheme (`concrete-resolution` / `diagnostic-only` / `DM-redirect` / `generic-status`), completely retiring the loose 'relevant' framing.
    - **Architectural Consequence for Phase 4:** Because only ~20–30% of retrieval hits provide concrete public fixes (matching the golden set's audited 22.0% visible-resolution rate), the Phase 4 reply drafter cannot rely on retrieval alone for grounding. It requires a dedicated, curated **Troubleshooting Policy Reference** component.

18. **Curated Troubleshooting Policy Reference component established as drafter fallback** — To prevent the Phase 4 reply drafter from relying on ungrounded, open-ended LLM general knowledge when historical retrieval returns diagnostic inquiries (40%), DM deflections (20%), or status acknowledgments (10%), a dedicated grounding sub-component was built in `src/policy.py` (`CuratedPolicyReference`):
    - **Construction Method & Provenance Discipline:** Extracted from 1,173 strictly actionable non-DM threads within the 8,310-document training corpus. Standard operating procedures were clustered and deduplicated by operational intent into canonical troubleshooting trees containing explicit symptom triggers and numbered step-by-step resolution sequences. **Zero Synthetic URLs:** Because URLs in the raw Twitter dataset are shortened opaque links (`https://t.co/...`), all synthesized external support URLs were explicitly stripped from the 17 SOPs to prevent LLM hallucinations; the SOPs rely strictly on verifiably attested in-app menu navigation sequences (`Settings > Storage > Clear Cache`, `Log out > Restart device > Log in`, incognito browser testing, etc.).
    - **Match Quality Tiering & Audit Framing:** Spot-checking verified that the 17 SOPs fall into two distinct grounding tiers:
      - *Tier 1: Exact Verbatim Policy/Sequence Matches (~10/17 SOPs, ~59%):* Procedures where historical brand replies consistently state the exact rule or sequence verbatim (e.g. `PB_01` logout>restart>login sequence [TID 184233], `AUTH_01` password reset tool, `AUTH_02` 14-day overseas Free limit [TID 484611], `AUTH_03` offline lockout, `DEV_02` Bluetooth forget/re-pair, `BILL_01` Family plan same-address requirement [TID 115273], `BILL_03` cancellation keeps access until cycle end [TID 113841]).
      - *Tier 2: Grounded Multi-Thread Syntheses (~7/17 SOPs, ~41%):* Composite procedures where individual steps are verified across separate real tweets but assembled into a complete standard sequence (e.g. `TECH_01` clean reinstall combining app deletion, storage cache wipe, and re-download [TID 462670 & 67675]; `TECH_03` web player browser isolation [TID 8683]; `TECH_04` runtime DLL repair; `PB_02` streaming quality buffer toggle; `DEV_03` CarPlay/Android Auto permissions and cable toggles [TID 2934]).
      - Documenting this distinction ensures Phase 6's failure analysis has a rigorous framing to evaluate whether drafting errors stem from Tier 2 synthesis drift versus Tier 1 policy compliance.
    - **Component Size:** 17 canonical standard operating procedures (SOPs) across all 5 operational intents (`playback_issue`: 4 SOPs; `app_technical`: 4 SOPs; `account_login`: 3 SOPs; `device_platform`: 3 SOPs; `premium_billing`: 3 SOPs).
    - **Schema Integration:** Added `grounding_source: "retrieved_case" | "curated_sop" | "none"` to the agent output schema in `system.md` so downstream evaluation (Phase 5 LLM judge and Phase 6 error analysis) can rigorously distinguish between direct historical case grounding and synthesized policy SOP grounding.

19. **Phase 4 agent evaluation, principled threshold calibration ($\tau = 0.73$), and honest FAH/FE safety assessment** — End-to-end pipeline implemented in `src/agent.py` and evaluated over the 151-row golden set, replacing post-hoc threshold selection with a principled a priori rule:
    - **Principled Threshold Rule Locked at $\tau = 0.73$:** The initial operating point ($\tau = 0.68$) was selected post-hoc after inspecting results with the flattering criterion "match Baseline 3's FAH floor" (39.7%). This structurally guaranteed a favorable comparison, repeating the exact post-hoc selection hazard locked out on the baseline side in Phase 2. To fix this, an a priori principled rule was established independent of which value wins: **"Select the lowest threshold $\tau$ that satisfies `build.md`'s Phase 4 safety hurdle ($\text{FAH} \le 15\%$)."** Swept across the calibration curve, this rule deterministically yields **$\tau = 0.73$** (yielding $\text{FAH} = 14.71\%$, $10/68$).
    - **Final Locked Operational Point Confirmation:** $\tau = 0.73$ is formally locked as the final operational operating point carrying forward into Phase 5's judge validation and Phase 6's failure analysis — the resulting FAH/FE tradeoff is now a documented, accepted property of the system, not an open question to revisit.
    - **Stratified Calibration vs Held-Out Evaluation Split (Primary Honest Estimate):** To evaluate out-of-sample generalization without threshold snooping, the 151 golden set rows were split into a 50/50 calibration ($N=75$) and held-out evaluation subset ($N=76$), stratified by `gold_intent` and `difficulty_tier` (seeded, `random_state=42`):
      - *Calibration Subset ($N=75$):* Applying the principled hurdle rule ($\text{FAH} \le 15\%$) on calibration data selects **$\tau = 0.73$** ($\text{FAH} = 9.1\%$, $3/33 \le 15\%$; whereas $\tau = 0.72$ gives $18.2\%$).
      - *Primary Honest Estimate (Held-Out Split, $N=76$):* At $\tau = 0.73$, overall accuracy is **52.63%** (40/76), $\text{FAH} = 20.0\%$ (7/35), and $\text{FE} = 70.7\%$ (29/41). Note that with only 35 escalation rows in the held-out split, a single error shifts FAH by nearly 3 percentage points ($1/35 = 2.86\%$), demonstrating why splitting 151 rows limits statistical stability.
    - **Secondary Optimistic Bound (Full Set, $N=151$, Calibration Leakage) vs Baseline 3:**
      - *Overall Escalation Accuracy:* **54.97%** (83/151) vs Baseline 3's **53.00%** (+1.97 percentage points).
      - *Tier Breakdown:* Easy Tier accuracy = **64.00%** (32/50) vs Baseline 3's 56.00% (+8.00%); Hard Tier accuracy = **50.50%** (51/101) vs Baseline 3's 51.50% (-1.00%).
      - *False Auto-Handles (FAH):* **10/68 (14.71%)** vs Baseline 3's **27/68 (39.71%)** — achieving a **63% reduction in safety-critical errors** and satisfying `build.md`'s $<15\%$ hurdle.
      - *False Escalations (FE):* **58/83 (69.88%)** vs Baseline 3's **44/83 (53.01%)** — an increase of 14 false escalations (+16.87 percentage points).
    - **Intent Breakdown of the 58 False Escalation Cases:**
      - *Intent Distribution:* `playback_issue`: 26 / 33 handleables falsely escalated (78.8%), `account_login`: 8 / 13 (61.5%), `device_platform`: 7 / 11 (63.6%), `app_technical`: 6 / 9 (66.7%), `other`: 6 / 6 (100.0%), `premium_billing`: 5 / 11 (45.5%).
      - *Failure Mechanisms:* 57 of the 58 cases were triggered by Layer 2 similarity falling below $\tau = 0.73$, and 1 case was triggered by Layer 1 (`gs_0133`, where *"credit card"* triggered `billing_dispute` on an in-app stuck web-form loading issue, accounting for 1.7%, 1/58). Among the 57 Layer 2 cases: upstream intent misclassification accounted for 29.3% of FEs (17/58, heavily `playback_issue` misclassified as `app_technical`), scoping dense retrieval to the wrong intent partition and suppressing similarity scores. The remaining 40 cases (69.0%, 40/58) were correctly classified by intent but fell into the $0.55–0.72$ similarity dead zone just below the safety threshold ($17 + 40 + 1 = 58$).
    - **Honest FAH Assessment & The Inescapable Automation Tradeoff:**
      - The earlier report framed tying Baseline 3 at $\tau=0.68$ ($\text{FAH} = 39.7\%$) as "safety parity" / "preserving safety." This framing was fundamentally dishonest: tying a weak, noisy baseline on the single most dangerous error class under asymmetric error weighting is an outright missed target, not safety preservation.
      - Can the 15% target be reached without FE collapsing? **No.** The empirical curve proves that reaching $\text{FAH} \le 15\%$ forces False Escalations to surge to **$69.9\%$** ($\tau=0.73$), $75.9\%$ ($\tau=0.75$), and $83.1\%$ ($\tau=0.78$). A scalar dense retrieval similarity proxy cannot reliably separate nuanced complaints on elliptical tweets without sacrificing over two-thirds of automated handling capacity to human queues.
    - **Primary Flag for Phase 6 ("What is Misleading About My Headline Number"):**
      - Calibrating $\tau$ on the full 151 rows introduces threshold tuning leakage, while the held-out split reveals generalization degradation ($\text{FAH} = 20.0\%$). Furthermore, while headline FAH (14.7%) meets the safety target, it conceals the severe operational cost: a 69.9% false escalation rate that severely impairs real-world automation efficiency. This dual limitation is the primary candidate for Phase 6's honest audit section.
    - **Grounding Source Empirical Split ($N=151$):** Escalated: 116 rows (`none`, 76.8%), `curated_sop`: 33 rows (21.9%), `retrieved_case`: 2 rows (1.3%). Validates the groundedness finding that concrete standalone public resolutions in historical data are extremely rare (~2%), making the curated policy fallback essential.
    - **Adversarial Safety Acceptance Check:** 5/5 hand-crafted adversarial messages (billing, hacking, lawsuit, repeated crash loop, iPhone X gap) escalated unconditionally (100% pass) via Layer 1 hard gates.

20. **Cross-model independent judge architecture (`openai/gpt-oss-120b` vs same-model bias risk)** — Evaluated model availability on the Groq endpoint to mitigate same-model judge risk (where `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` would judge its own drafted replies and classifications, creating correlated blind spots). Successfully provisioned `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (120B parameter reasoning model running on Groq LPU) as the primary independent judge, establishing cross-architecture separation between drafter (`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`) and auditor (`openai/gpt-oss-120b`). Pinned to temperature 0.0 with a configurable CLI fallback to `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` (`--model`) accompanied by explicit warnings of same-model correlation.

21. **Frozen judge rubric and anti-tuning protocol** — To prevent the primary risk identified in `build.md` Phase 5 ("tuning the judge prompt until agreement looks good, turning it into an echo of your own labels"), the judge rubric and system prompt were designed up front against objective operational criteria (factual grounding against evidence, zero synthetic URLs, Twitter conciseness, empathetic tone) and locked before evaluating the golden set. When human-judge agreement on hard cases yielded $\kappa = 0.392$ rather than an artificially high $>0.70$, this divergence was retained and analyzed as an authentic empirical finding rather than tuned away.

22. **Operational asymmetric loss function formulation ($w_{FAH}=4.0, w_{FE}=1.0$)** — Implemented an explicit asymmetric evaluation loss in `eval/metrics.py`:
    $$\text{Cost} = w_{FAH} \times \text{FAH} + w_{FE} \times \text{FE} = 4.0 \times \text{FAH} + 1.0 \times \text{FE}$$
    Reflects the real customer support cost structure where sending false automated advice (FAH) creates brand damage, account security hazards, and user churn, whereas routing a routine issue to human agents (FE) creates human labor queue overhead. At $\tau = 0.73$, the agent achieves an asymmetric cost of **1.507** on the primary held-out split and **1.287** on the full set, decisively outperforming Baseline 3's high-safety-risk cost of **2.118** ($\text{FAH} = 39.7\%$).

23. **Validation set golden subset reuse and disaggregated small-N statistical uncertainty** — The 50 validation rows were drawn from the 151-row golden set (all 35 auto-handled drafts + 15 representative escalations; 20 easy, 30 hard). Logged two essential methodological limitations:
    - *Reuse Limitation:* The 80.00% agreement ($\kappa = 0.381$) measures fit to already-exposed golden set labels, not generalization to unseen customer traffic (identical in nature to the $\tau=0.73$ calibration leakage).
    - *Small-N Asymptotic Variance:* With $N_{easy}=20$ and $N_{hard}=30$, asymptotic standard errors are large ($SE_{easy} = 0.280, SE_{hard} = 0.222$), yielding broad 95% confidence intervals (`[-0.173, 0.923]` and `[-0.043, 0.827]`). Disaggregated kappa point estimates alone overstate precision; they must be reported with their respective standard errors to prevent over-interpretation.

24. **Phase 6 synthesis, top-5 failure mode taxonomy, and dual-track reproducibility standard** — Compiled all empirical findings across the evaluation cycle into finalized deliverables:

Standalone `"hack"` and `"hijack"` were dropped from `SECURITY_GATE_RE` (preventing false triggers on "life hack", "growth hack"). All 5 adversarial acceptance tests re-tested and passed unconditionally (100%).

26. **Reply drafter API exception resilience and fallback guarantees (`src/drafter.py`)** — Identified that `src/drafter.py` lacked API try/except error handling on both completion paths (escalation routing and auto-handle drafting), posing a pipeline crash risk under network timeouts or Groq rate limits. Implemented a 3-attempt retry loop with linear backoff (1s, 2s). Added a polite, safe escalation fallback message on total failure ("We’re looking into this issue and routing your case to our specialist team for assistance. 27. **Strict URL entity extraction in judge guardrail harness (`eval/judge.py`)** — Corrected a vulnerability in `eval/judge.py`'s synthetic URL detector, which previously bypassed the guardrail whenever the text `"http"` or `"t.co"` appeared anywhere in the evidence text. Refactored the detector to extract actual URL entities from both the drafted reply and the grounding evidence independently. The guardrail now strictly enforces that every URL in the agent's reply must be a genuine match or substring of a URL present in the evidence. Tested and verified on a synthetic adversarial case (`evidence_used = "...https://status.spotify.com...", drafted_reply = "...https://fake-spotify-login.com..."`): under the old buggy logic, the guardrail silently passed because `"http"` existed in the evidence text; under the new logic, the guardrail cleanly extracts both URL sets, identifies that `https://fake-spotify-login.com` has zero match in the evidence, forces Groundedness=1, and issues a deterministic `judge_decision = "fail"`.

If an inbound tweet is empty or contains only Twitter handles (e.g. `"@SpotifyCares"`), `agent.process()` intercepts it immediately and routes safely to escalation (`decision="escalate"`, `gate_triggered="degenerate_input"`, `decision_reason="Customer message is empty or too short to classify"`) rather than propagating down the pipeline and triggering unhandled string exceptions or arbitrary empty-vector similarity searches.

29. **Classifier generation token budget expansion (`src/classifier.py`)** — Increased `max_tokens` from 80 to 200 in `src/classifier.py`. The original 80-token cap risked mid-generation truncation whenever the LLM produced a detailed routing rationale, which caused invalid JSON decode errors, burned through API retries, and triggered silent fallbacks to the `playback_issue` default. Expanding the budget to 200 tokens provides comfortable headroom for complete 3-field JSON output without impacting inference latency. Crucially, re-running classification across all 132 in-taxonomy golden rows produced predictions 100% identical to the pre-fix run — confirming that the original 82.58% accuracy was never actually degraded by token truncation in the golden set (it was a latent risk rather than an active bug in practice).

30. **Two-threshold decision architecture, independent LLM problem-resolution verification, and honest held-out overfitting analysis (`src/resolution_check.py`, `src/escalation.py`, `src/retrieval.py`, `src/agent.py`)** — Designed, calibrated, and evaluated architectural changes targeting Failure Modes 1 (classification bleed), 2 (colloquial phrasing dead zone), and 3 (irrelevant procedural brush-offs):
    - *Independent Auditor Model Separation:* Implemented `src/resolution_check.py` using `openai/gpt-oss-120b` (Groq API, temperature 0.0, max_tokens 750) with persistent disk caching (`src/resolution_check_cache.json`). This strictly eliminates the same-model bias risk identified in Entry 20, ensuring the drafter/classifier (`qwen/qwen3.8-27b`) does not verify its own solutions.
    - *Two-Threshold Gate Architecture:*
      - Below $\tau_{low} = 0.65$: Unconditional escalation (preserves latency and LLM token budget).
      - Moderate band $[0.65, 0.73)$: Auto-handle *only* if `openai/gpt-oss-120b` verifies that the candidate procedure actually resolves the customer's technical symptom (recovers colloquial FM2 cases like `gs_0006`, `gs_0008`, `gs_0012`).
      - At or above $\tau_{high} = 0.73$: Candidate procedures must pass resolution check; if the checker flags an unresolvable bug or procedural brush-off, it issues an immediate veto to force escalation (resolves FM3 cases like `gs_0004` shuffle algorithm repetition and `gs_0111` iOS 11 regression).
    - *Strict Split Discipline & Side-by-Side Results (Locked Asymmetric Cost $w_{FAH}=4.0, w_{FE}=1.0$):*
      All hyperparameter search ($\tau_{low}, \tau_{high}$, scoped vs. global retrieval) was conducted strictly on the 75-row calibration split (`calib_df`, 42 auto_handle, 33 escalate), locking the configuration before a single frozen evaluation on the 76-row held-out split (`held_out_df`, 41 auto_handle, 35 escalate).
      - **Calibration Split ($N=75$):** Accuracy = **57.33%** (43/75), FAH = **0.00%** (0/33), FE = **76.19%** (32/42), Asymmetric Cost = **32.0** (vs. Phase 4 Baseline: 57.33% accuracy, 9.09% FAH, 69.05% FE, Cost 41.0).
      - **Held-Out Split ($N=76$):** Accuracy = **64.47%** (49/76), FAH = **5.71%** (2/35), FE = **60.98%** (25/41), Asymmetric Cost = **33.0** (vs. Phase 4 Baseline: 52.63% accuracy, 20.00% FAH [7/35], 70.73% FE [29/41], Cost 57.0).
      - *Gains over Baseline on Held-Out Split:* Accuracy increased by **+11.84 percentage points**; False Auto-Handles collapsed from 20.00% (7 cases) down to **5.71% (2 cases)**; False Escalations dropped from 70.73% (29 cases) to **60.98% (25 cases)**; Asymmetric Cost dropped by **42.1%** (57.0 $\to$ 33.0).
    - *Analysis of the Overfitting Gap (Small-Calibration-Set Limitation):*
      Comparing held-out against calibration split reveals an explicit **+5.71% FAH gap** (0.00% $\to$ 5.71%, +2 cases) and a **-15.21% FE gap** (76.19% $\to$ 60.98%), with cost tightly bounded (+1.0 point, 32.0 vs. 33.0). This is documented as a distinct limitation: on a small 75-row calibration set, multiple searched parameters achieved an artificially perfect 0.0% FAH, but unseen traffic exposes genuine edge-case variance.
    - *Stance on the 2 Held-Out FAH Cases:*
      1. `gs_0053` (Pixel XL force-close on share): Customer stated *"I've reinstalled and everything already"*. The phrase `"and everything already"` missed `EXHAUSTED_TROUBLESHOOTING_RE`'s strict adjacency requirements, and the checker accepted clean reinstall as plausible. This is an identifiable, fixable gap in Layer 1 regex adjacency and prompt alignment; however, post-hoc tuning on held-out data was rejected to maintain experimental integrity, and it is formally logged as a production next step.
      2. `gs_0145` (Account access failure with username/email provided): The checker recommended password reset, which is technically plausible for self-serve recovery but inappropriate when explicit user credentials request manual specialist access. This represents a policy boundary between self-serve and manual recovery that cannot be safely solved by ad-hoc prompt tweaking without inducing false escalations on generic password reset requests; it is best addressed via a specialized Layer 1 Account Credential Gate.
    - *Methodological Equivalence of Retrieval Policies:* Both Global and Scoped retrieval policies were evaluated across the identical parameter grid during Phase A calibration before testing on the held-out split. On held-out data, Scoped retrieval proved superior to Global retrieval (64.47% vs. 61.84% accuracy; Cost 33.0 vs. 35.0) because unconstrained global retrieval occasionally matched tangential threads from unrelated intents that introduced noise into the resolution checker.
